01What We're Building

The direction has sharpened twice — worth being explicit about both moves.

LMX Cloud started as a DePIN inference router: an OpenAI-compatible API that routes AI inference requests across decentralized GPU networks (io.net, Akash) instead of a single centralized provider, with automatic failover if one network degrades.

The positioning has since sharpened into something narrower and more defensible: LMX Cloud is Web3-native infrastructure built specifically for autonomous AI agents — software that holds its own crypto wallet and pays for its own compute with no human in the loop. The pitch is two things bolted together, not one: x402-native agent payments, paired with routing to decentralized compute supply a centralized neocloud has no reason to ever build toward.

End state (Phase 1): an agent with zero prior relationship to LMX discovers it via x402 Bazaar, MCP, or the ElizaOS plugin, pays per call in stablecoin, and gets routed DePIN compute back — no signup, no credit card, no human in the loop.

02The Thesis — Why This, Not Something More Obvious

Two roads that look easier both dead-end. The actual wedge is between them.

Road not taken — cheap GPU for everyone

Already a red ocean

CoreWeave alone: $23B IPO valuation, $5B 2025 revenue, $99.4B order backlog by March 2026 — competing purely on price and capacity. 300+ new GPU cloud entrants launched in 2025; H100 rental prices fell 64–75% in eighteen months. Capital-intensive, already owned by well-funded centralized players.

Road not taken — decentralized compute for "web3 devs"

Smaller and more stagnant than it looks

Akash, the closest comparable, sells to a handful of crypto-native projects who mostly sell to each other — and its active provider count recently hit the lowest point in the network's history.

The actual wedge: autonomous agents and DAOs that structurally cannot use hyperscaler billing. A DAO's treasury is a multisig, not a corporate entity — it can't open an AWS enterprise account. An agent can't sign an enterprise services agreement. Both can hold and spend a crypto wallet. x402 (backed by Coinbase, Cloudflare, Stripe, Google, Microsoft, AWS, Circle, Visa, Mastercard) is becoming the way software pays software — and pairing that rail with decentralized compute is something a centralized neocloud would have to give up margin and control to build.

04How We Make Money

Not like CoreWeave, which owns hardware and carries capex. LMX Cloud owns no compute — it buys wholesale from io.net and Akash and charges a spread per request. That's closer to Stripe/Plaid economics than a cloud provider's: thin margin per unit, no capex, and margin that holds or improves as the underlying DePIN market keeps getting cheaper.

Revenue scales by routing more traffic through the toll booth — not by building more capacity.

Three systems stitched together: inference routing, identity/payments, and agent-facing distribution.

Identity & payments — two paths, same balance system

Funded path: sign in by email (Clerk) or wallet (SIWE / EIP-4361) — including a raw keypair with no browser, which is what makes an agent able to mint its own key. Deposit USDC on Base to a treasury address; an on-chain listener confirms and auto-credits balance. Every call draws down that balance.

Pay-per-call path (x402): no key at all. An agent calls the endpoint, gets 402 Payment Required, signs a payment payload, retries. Coinbase's CDP Facilitator verifies and settles the USDC payment on Base, LMX routes the request, and the whole thing is logged as a payment_event.

Trust layer

Every request produces a receipt; receipts are batched and Merkle-anchored on-chain (LmxLogAnchor on Base), with a public proof endpoint. The idea: routing, latency, and cost claims stay cryptographically checkable after the fact — this matters once the buyer is an anonymous agent with no relationship to fall back on.

09Moats & Risks

Honest accounting — what's actually defensible versus what looks like a moat but isn't.

● What's defensible

Verifiable trust layerOn-chain receipt anchoring is live and working today — matters specifically once the buyer is an anonymous agent with no relationship to fall back on.
Measured reliability dataPersisted provider-health history across independent DePIN networks becomes a genuine, hard-to-fake content asset once it has weeks of depth — no competitor currently publishes this.
Distribution head startLive on the official MCP registry and x402 Bazaar with a real mainnet-settled transaction history and an honest public case study (the MCP "stranger walkthrough" bug story).
The wedge itselfHyperscalers have no margin incentive to route to decentralized supply; DAOs/agents structurally can't use hyperscaler billing. That combination, not "decentralization," is the actual defensible position.

● What isn't a moat

The payment rail itselfx402 is an open standard — nothing stops io.net or Akash from adding direct support and disintermediating an aggregator layer like LMX.
Thin, lumpy demandA meaningful share of early x402 activity industry-wide has come from one viral memecoin experiment, not organic diverse demand.
Underlying DePIN healthThe resilience pitch depends on DePIN supply staying healthy — Akash's active provider count is at its lowest point on record.
Provider concentrationOnly two DePIN providers live in production today; "resource-agnostic routing" isn't a real claim yet without more provider depth.
Why this matters: none of the above are reasons to not pursue the bet — they're the specific signals that would tell us early if it isn't working. That's why Phase 2 is explicitly gated on a real unprompted external payment, not a feature checklist.
