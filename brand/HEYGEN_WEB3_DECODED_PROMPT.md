# Web3, Decoded — LMX Cloud video generation brief

**Series:** Web3, Decoded (Episode 01) — a recurring LMX Cloud series pairing one Web3 principle with the exact piece of the product built on it. The open and close in this brief are the locked series template; future episodes reuse them unedited and only swap the content scenes.

I need a ~4 minute educational explainer video: five Web3 principles, and exactly where LMX Cloud has already built each one. Follow these constraints exactly.

## Hard constraints

* No avatar. No talking head. No presenter on screen at any point. Faceless, narration-over-motion-graphics — same format as the last two LMX videos.
* No stock footage of people. No office b-roll, no typing at laptops, no handshakes, no generic corporate imagery.
* No generic crypto/AI imagery. No glowing brains, no generic neural-mesh art, no Bitcoin/Ethereum coin icons, no robot hands, no circuit boards, no "hacker in a hoodie." The **one exception is LMX's own wireframe-cloud mark and its node/line visual language** — that's brand, not cliché, and it should be the recurring motif (see Visual direction).
* 16:9 landscape, 1920×1080.
* Use the LMX Cloud brand system below for all color, type, and logo — no substitutions.

## Color system

These are hard rules, not preferences.

* **One theme: dark, throughout.** The LMX mark is a glowing wireframe cloud designed for dark surfaces — `BRAND_GUIDELINES.md` explicitly says don't place it on light. No light-surface scenes anywhere in the video. Pacing comes from three tonal layers instead of a theme switch:
  * **Void** `#0a0b0f` — title card, closing card, and the one "why now" tonal-shift scene. Nothing else uses Void.
  * **Surface** `#14151c` — the standard content scenes (the five principle scenes).
  * **Elevated** `#1e2029` — agenda and assembled-model scenes, where content needs to read as "raised" above the base layer.
* Maximum three colors per scene: base, ink, and one accent.
* Exactly one highlighted word or element per scene. Named explicitly in each scene below. A second highlight cancels the first.
* **Secondary cyan `#3dd7e5`** is the highlight color and the logo's own glow color. It appears exactly once per scene, on the one thing that scene wants remembered. Never use it for body text or structural elements — if it's everywhere, it stops meaning anything.
* **Primary indigo `#5b6bff`** is structural, not a highlight: card borders on the "LMX Cloud, today" panels, connecting lines in diagrams, the wallet/agent/vault/router card outlines. Never use indigo for the one-highlight rule above — that's cyan's job only.
* **Tertiary `#f5d547`** appears exactly once in the entire video — the "why now" scene (Scene 10) — and nowhere else. If it shows up twice, the urgency beat is gone.
* **Success green `#2be08c`** is reserved for the checkmark motif on things that are actually shipped. Never decorative, never used for anything still in development.
* Text is `#f2f4f8` (primary) or `#9aa0ae` (muted) on any of the three base layers — both are pre-verified for contrast on all three. Never set indigo `#5b6bff` as body text color against Void `#0a0b0f`; the two are too close in value and the text disappears. Indigo is for shapes and lines only, never for reading text.
* No gradients on large fills. Solid color only — gradients band at 1080p and don't survive re-compression once this gets uploaded to LinkedIn/X.

## Typography

Brand guidelines don't lock in a type system beyond "bold, sentence case" for the wordmark, so this brief defines one:

* Headlines in **Inter Bold**, sentence case.
* Labels and eyebrows in **Inter Medium**, uppercase, wide letter spacing (matches the "PRINCIPLE 1 OF 5" / "LMX CLOUD, TODAY" kicker style already established in LMX's own slide decks — carry that convention into this video).
* Body in **Inter Regular**.
* Scene titles at roughly 64px, body at 32px, nothing on screen smaller than 24px — this gets watched on phones (X, LinkedIn feed autoplay), so err large.
* Keep readable text inside a 160px margin from the left and right edges, and keep the bottom 180px of frame clear (captions get burned in later by the platform, or added from the separate SRT).

## Logo usage

* Full lockup (icon + "LMX Cloud" wordmark) appears only in the title card and the closing card — nowhere else.
* Every content scene (2 through 10) carries the icon-only mark, small, bottom-right corner, at reduced opacity (~50%), paired with a two-digit scene number in Inter Medium, faint gray `#5c6170` — a consistent footer band in the same corner every scene. This exact pairing (page number + small logo icon, bottom-right) is already the convention in LMX's own slide decks; reuse it here for cross-format consistency.
* Never render the icon below the equivalent of 32px at delivery resolution — the triangulated detail degrades into noise smaller than that.

## Visual direction

Think developer-keynote product teaser, not a corporate explainer — confident, high-contrast, precise, still with room to breathe. A frame can be 60% empty and still feel finished; typography and the node-line motif do the work, not decoration.

* **The recurring motif is LMX's own visual language: small nodes connected by thin lines**, drawn in cyan at low opacity. Use it three ways: (1) a quiet drifting background texture on the title and closing cards, (2) the literal diagram style for every concept that needs illustrating — decentralization is drawn as nodes routing around a removed node, the assembled model is drawn as connected boxes — and (3) never as a random decoration disconnected from what's being said.
* Strong left alignment on all content scenes. Centered only on the title and closing cards.
* Every content scene splits into two labeled halves where relevant: "WEB3 PRINCIPLE" (left, muted ink, plain) and "LMX CLOUD, TODAY" (right, cyan label, indigo-bordered card) — this mirrors the structure LMX's own explainer deck already uses, so don't redesign it, animate it.

## Motion

* Fade for text: ~0.25s.
* Slide-up for cards and panels: ~0.25s.
* Pop for numerals, highlights, and checkmarks: ~0.15s.
* Node/line diagrams drawing themselves in (nodes appearing, then lines connecting them): ~0.4s.
* Nothing animates longer than 0.4s.
* Time every element to the specific word in the narration that it illustrates, not to a fixed interval. The trigger word is named in each scene.

## Voice and pacing

Confident, clear, technical-but-accessible US English. Moderate-to-brisk pace — an engineer who built this explaining it plainly, not a hype voiceover. No sales language, no exclamation points in tone. Use the script verbatim: do not rewrite, shorten, or add lines. Where a pause is marked, insert an actual pause of that length.
